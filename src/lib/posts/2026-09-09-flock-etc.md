Flock cameras have entered the political discourse in Louisiana. The Livingston Parish council is [debating a measure](https://www.theadvocate.com/ordinance-to-restrict-flock-cameras-pushed-back-to-committee-in-livingston-parish/article_55fd793c-2e99-4e2b-984a-26aaca47129c.html) to restrict their usage. State Representative Danny McCormick is [pushing a bill](https://www.wdsu.com/article/flock-ban-law-louisiana/73430656) to ban Flock cameras across the state. Baton Rouge Police have attempted to get ahead of the issue by [defending their use](https://www.wafb.com/2026/08/10/baton-rouge-police-defend-flock-cameras-privacy-concerns-mount-nationwide/) of Flock cameras for the last 5 years. 

The concern about Flock cameras is warranted, since Jefferson Parish deputy Nathan Rome used JPSO's surveillance apparatus to [stalk his ex](https://www.wwltv.com/article/news/local/jefferson/jpso-deputy-who-tracked-ex-with-flock-cameras-thousands-of-times-identified/289-3e7c3279-5df5-4243-9bbb-cc9ffd9eaa36). His is not the only case in the US. Flock cameras are called Automatic License Plate Readers (ALPRs) although they do more than simply scan license plates. The cameras take photos of vehicles, and algorithms can be used to track individual cars or traffic patterns. The data, including the photos that can include drivers and passengers, are typically owned and stored by police departments. This all raises concerns about privacy, free speech, and freedom of movement. However, as socialists, we must look beyond the immediate problem and look to the systems that create it. Flock is not the only company in the ALPR market and ALPRs are not the only way police track people. Nor are local police the only ones using technology to increase their surveillance on society. Most of all, Flock's contracts with local police is only the latest in merging of corporate interests into government operations. 

[Earlier this year](https://www.wbrz.com/news/baton-rouge-police-department-says-it-s-the-first-department-in-country-to-use-new-type-of-drone/), the Baton Rouge Police Department added an Edge Autonomy Stalker VXE30 to its 30 drone arsenal. It is a military drone complete with thermal cameras and a 16 foot wingspan. Not to be outdone, the West Baton Rouge Sheriff's office added four drones and a "robo dog" named SPOT. District Attorney for the 18th Judicial District [Tony Clayton said](https://www.theadvocate.com/baton_rouge/news/crime_police/west-baton-rouge-iberville-tony-clayton/article_c422a8ce-5463-4f68-9da4-97fbc4adda59.html) 

> I don't believe government should be watching people, _but_ for that 1% who operate in the criminal world, Big Brother will be watching you. We know where your little drug hangouts are, we know who the gang members are, and we're going to be monitoring, so those are the people that have to worry, not people like you and me.

Clayton's comment is revealing. He believes there are two worlds: one for "people like you and me" and one for criminals. Criminals, obviously, do not deserve rights to privacy or the 4th amendment. Not only does he believe criminals are not people, but he knows who the criminals are, and therefore can ensure that only *their* rights are violated. Rights are for people like you and me. If Clayton already knows who is a criminal, then why doesn't he just arrest them now? Why does he need the surveillance drones to track them if he already knows they are criminals?

The answer is that Clayton does not actually know, but is happy to rely on the judgment (read: racism) of cops to determine who is and isn't worthy of rights. He is also lying, because he later reveals that the drones will be present at parades and football games and other large gatherings. We have heard this before. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) has likewise relied on racism to determine who is and isn't worthy of due process rights. Supreme Court Justice [Brett Kavanaugh granted law enforcement](https://timesofsandiego.com/opinion/2026/01/11/thanks-to-lapd-decades-ago-law-enforcement-abuse-is-protected/), ICE in particular, the right to harass people because of their "apparent ethnicity" which proliferated so-called "Kavanaugh Stops". President Donald Trump has attempted to [suspend Habeas Corpus](https://newrepublic.com/post/211828/trump-close-suspend-constitutional-right-habeas-corpus), which would allow ICE to detain anyone without explanation. Suspending Habeas Corpus would be a mere formality at this point, considering the detention of Mahmoud Khalil, the deportation of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, and the street execution of Alex Pretti. Maybe they were not "people like you and me".

In this context, Flock cameras are just the latest in an erosion of human rights under American fascism. They are also more than that. As Martin Luther King Jr. once said, "The bombs in Vietnam explode at home, they destroy the hopes and possibilities for a decent America." Flock is a part of the [everything problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycrisis). Israel has long used US technology to [heavily surveil Palestinians](https://mei.edu/publication/nowhere-hide-impact-israels-digital-surveillance-regime-palestinians/) in Gaza and the West Bank. And tech billionaire Peter Theil of [Palantir](https://afsc.org/palantir-explainer), became connected to Israel through Jeffrey Epstein! While AI missiles are used by the US and Israel to target Iran, the data centers that power those missiles are destroying communities in [Richland Parish](https://fortune.com/2026/03/26/meta-ai-data-center-hyperion-louisiana/) and across the US. The same companies funding and profiting from the data center and AI boom are the same ones pushing to [remove SpaceX's liability](https://lailluminator.com/2026/08/25/wildlife-spacex/) for destroying homes and habitats in Louisiana. At the same time, we are experiencing a climate catastrophe that is making Louisiana increasingly unlivable due to heat, rising sea levels, and polluting of our water. Their solution to this problem is to pollute further by [injecting the carbon](https://lailluminator.com/2026/02/11/louisianas-nation-leading-proposed-carbon-capture-projects-alarm-environmentalists/) they release into the ground. 

The deeper you look, the more obvious it becomes that the revolt against Flock cameras results from a revolt against an entire system. The system is one where the desires of the rich are privileged over the desires of the whole population. A system where democracy is undermined for the sake of profits. A system that increasingly divides the world into those who control everything and those who control nothing, not even their own lives. A system that connects police mass surveillance, expensive data centers, unwelcome carbon capture and storage, increasingly rising costs, the Epstein files, Israel's genocide, senseless wars, and the general feeling that things are getting worse. That system is called Capitalism. 

In the Democratic Socialists of America, we believe that a better world is possible. That better world is one where democracy, not wealth, prevails. Where crime is addressed at its roots in poverty and excess, not with surveillance and reaction. Where human rights are respected for *all* people. Because there is only one world. Capitalism is tearing it apart.

As the Baton Rouge Democratic Socialists of America, we demand

- An end to the militarization of police and their technologies
- Redistributing funds from the police for community solutions to the root causes of crime
- A ban on Nondisclosure Agreements (NDAs) by public officials with tech companies
