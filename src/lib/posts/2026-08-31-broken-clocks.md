For months, the inhumane conditions at the Dilley Detention Center in Texas have been coming across my social media feeds. I’ve read letters from kids who feel that they are in prison. I’ve read accounts from parents saying that their children have started wetting the bed again, have begun to self-harm, and have even tried to take their own lives. The stories are difficult and often harrowing to read. And yet, after increasing publicity about the poor conditions at Dilley and public demands that it be shut down, we in Louisiana have found ourselves needing to pivot our organizing efforts toward protesting the opening of yet another of these "family detention facilities" – the Alexandria Child Detention Center located at the Alexandria International Airport. 

Detention centers in America are only one of the many struggles for liberation we are currently facing. But unlike past movements, our generation is able to look through our phones directly into the eyes of the oppressed in real time and see the horrors being wrought on our neighbors at the hands of our government, both at home and abroad. Rather than shocking the world into radical defiance, livestreaming the atrocities has only gone to normalize the banality of evil. I, a now-pregnant woman surrounded by a supportive and loving family, look through the mirror every day at women whose lives couldn’t be more different from my own, and yet whose only real separation from me is the circumstances of our births. The black mother attending the funeral of her lynched son, the Palestinian mother holding her dead child in her arms, the pregnant woman in ICE detention who fears for the health of her unborn child. They are all my mother, my sister, myself. 

I find comfort in knowing that our struggles today are not so unlike those of our spiritual (and literal) ancestors who fought for abolition, labor rights, and civil rights. We are united in solidarity across time against the same evil that continues to rear its head through the many faces of colonialism, patriarchy, white supremacy, and capitalism. [Ralph Hennessy, the executive director](mailto:rhennessy@englandairpark.org) for the England Economic and Industrial Development District (which is the government entity responsible for the Alexandria Child Detention Center) made this all very plain when he [responded to recent protests](https://www.wbrz.com/news/alexandria-airport-expands-ice-detention-facilities-as-protesters-push-back/) by saying, "There are a lot of people in Central Louisiana that make a living off of the immigration world, the prison world."

I am rarely taken aback these days when the worst people in the world accidentally say, to use a tired phrase, "the quiet part out loud," but I found this statement to be surprisingly profound in its clarity and truth. To value the health, safety, and dignity of all people without regard for profit is to set oneself in opposition to what has always been the driver of the ruling and governing class: greed. Though this evil will mutate itself into many forms throughout decades and demographics, we must continue to ground our opposition in the simple, unwavering truth that profit is no determiner of value. A society is only as free as its most oppressed people. It is only as kind as the treatment of its enemies. It is only as wealthy as its most destitute. It is only as whole as its most broken.
